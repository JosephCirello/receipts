# The demo video, in words

For anyone, person or program, who would rather read the video than watch it. Everything said and everything shown, in order.

- **The video:** https://youtu.be/vLFWDG3o_xA (2 minutes 54 seconds)
- **What it is:** screen recordings of Receipts running, with a narrator. The product is on screen from 0:30.
- **Where it was recorded:** the video shows the runs on PayPal's real sandbox. The [hosted demo](https://receipts-4nvn.onrender.com) replays the same recorded runs on our stand-in for PayPal.
- **How it was made:** recorded with Playwright; the voice is a synthetic narrator; the music was made with an AI music model. The video names no AI model.

## In five sentences

Maya runs a small furniture workshop, and an AI agent pays her bills through PayPal. On Monday she pays a supplier $1,836; on Tuesday a second notice crosses that payment in the mail, the agent checks PayPal for completed payments, finds nothing, and pays the bill again. Receipts sits between the agent and PayPal, reads every payment status, holds the second payment, and asks Maya on her phone; she taps Decline and the money never leaves. Across 19 AI models, 19 business days and 1,444 runs, 7.0% of the money unguarded agents sent should never have left; with Receipts on the same days, $0.00 did, and 98% of honest payments went straight through. Receipts also enforces the owner's rules written in plain English, checks what the agent says against what it did, checks its end-of-day report against PayPal, and keeps every decision in one book.

## Where each number in the video comes from

Every figure the video states is in the [README](README.md) with its source, and `python media/stated_numbers.py` prints them from the recorded runs. The [judges' guide](JUDGES.md) says how to check each one. PayPal's loss rate (0.07% of payment volume in 2025) is from PayPal's own annual report, linked in the README; our 7.0% comes from a test with far more traps than a real month, as the README says.
