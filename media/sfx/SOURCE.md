# The two sound effects

Both are made from sine waves by `make_sfx.py` in this folder, with the Python standard library only. Nothing is sampled, downloaded or generated by a model, and running the script again writes the same two files byte for byte. 48 kHz, 16-bit, mono.

**`ding.wav`**, a small struck bell (the "ding" of a shop till), 0.75 seconds. Four sine waves added together: the main note at 1760 Hz (A6), two higher ones at 2.756 and 5.404 times that (4851 Hz and 9511 Hz, the pitches a struck metal bar rings at), and a quieter one an octave below (880 Hz) for body. Loudness 1.00, 0.40, 0.10 and 0.16. Each dies away on its own: 60 dB in 0.60, 0.38, 0.20 and 0.60 seconds. A 3 millisecond attack, the last 20 milliseconds eased to silence. Scaled so its loudest sample is 14 dB under full scale, about 10 dB under the narration's peaks.

**`tap.wav`**, a soft key press like a phone keyboard's, 0.08 seconds. Three sine waves: a low thump at 190 Hz and a short click at 1400 Hz and 3100 Hz, loudness 0.60, 0.50 and 0.20, each falling away exponentially (time constants 18, 8 and 4 milliseconds). A 1 millisecond attack, the last 10 milliseconds eased to silence. Scaled so its loudest sample is 16 dB under full scale.

Where they play is in `media/script.json` (a scene's `"sfx"` list): the bell on the frame the red figure changes to $1,836.00 in the opening and on the frame the red "Money moved" line arrives; the tap on the frame Decline is pressed on Maya's phone. `media/assemble.py` lays them into `receipts_demo_sfx.mp4`; `receipts_demo.mp4` is the same pictures with voice and music only.
